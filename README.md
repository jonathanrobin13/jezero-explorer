# Jezero Explorer

Jezero Explorer is a NASA Space Apps Challenge project to help visitors explore evidence of ancient water in Jezero Crater and plan a prototype terrain-aware Marswalk to a science destination.

Project planning and challenge requirements are in `resources/`. The `data/` directory holds dataset metadata. Downloaded raster payloads are kept outside Git history.

## Astronaut mission

Jezero Explorer opens on the **2D map**. Select **Start astronaut mission** to open the landing planner. Choose a touchdown point or select **Load western delta survey** to start at the Perseverance landing coordinates and navigate about 2.6 km to a simulated observation waypoint at the ancient delta. The scenario is based on the [reported landing coordinates](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230016428/downloads/Bell%20et%20al%202022sciadv.pdf) and [NASA's account of Perseverance's delta traverse](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/perseverances-route-to-the-delta-2/).

The intro shows powered descent, landing-gear deployment, touchdown, and the astronaut exiting the airlock and looking around. **Play landing and egress animation** defaults to the browser's motion preference. **Skip to visor** is available during the intro. Drag the visor or use the arrow keys to look. Use **WASD** to walk, or **W/S** to drive and **A/D** to steer the buggy. The navigation map sets a custom waypoint. **Set lander as waypoint** provides a bearing and distance back to the vehicle.

The first-person mission reuses the western CTX mesh at 1× vertical scale, up to 80 m between mesh vertices. The cyan outline marks this detailed survey. The game extends 4.8 km in each direction into the regional CTX elevation model and blends the two surfaces at the seam. The playable area is about 33.1 × 26.0 km. The visor reports the source, elevation, and grade of the displayed terrain triangle. With generated scenery or a data gap, it withholds the elevation and grade. The application uses simulated rocks, travel speeds, lighting and cutscenes. The mission is a rehearsal for selecting a destination and navigating terrain, and the display mesh can miss small hazards.

## Minerals and ancient-water evidence

The 2D map includes **13 hoverable science locations** for water-altered minerals and landforms associated with ancient water. Clicking the map recenters and zooms to that area; clicking a science marker opens its evidence. Each pop-up highlights **Key point** and **Why it matters** in bold. Regional place labels identify Jezero Crater, the Western and Northern deltas, and the Neretva, Sava, and Pliva valleys. Their positions are approximate label anchors, not mapped feature boundaries. In the astronaut simulation, the same science locations appear as small 3D ground markers and selectable stops; the regional names also appear on its navigation map. Selecting a stop sets it as the astronaut's waypoint. Mineral and water-evidence symbols use separate colors and symbols. The layer is labeled as regional evidence, not a map of exact mineral boundaries or present-day liquid water.

## Jezero weather archive

Select a location on the **2D map**, **Crater UV**, or the astronaut mission's touchdown or navigation map. During EVA, **Weather here** selects the astronaut's current coordinates. The panel reports CTX elevation at the selected point and distance to the Perseverance observation site. The Earth date and Mars local solar time follow the same observations as the 2D UV overlay.

The weather panel uses November 11–18, 2022 UTC to match the archived UV window. It pairs calibrated air temperature from MEDA ATS1 and derived pressure from MEDA PS with the UV snapshots within one spacecraft-clock second. Temperature is available for all 142 UV snapshots and pressure for 128. Missing pressure is shown as unavailable. Product identifiers, labels, versions, and download links are in the generated [weather archive](frontend/public/data/map/MEDA/jezero-weather.json).

Dust context comes from Mark Lemmon's [Mastcam-Z Visible Aerosol Optical Depth Record, version 2](https://doi.org/10.17632/gktjp9jptb.2), published by Mendeley Data under CC BY 4.0. This is 880 nm column optical depth above Perseverance, sampled from solar images. Atmospheric dust and clouds can both contribute. The panel shows opacity from the selected UTC date with its own observation time and distance. The exporter checks positions against MEDA ancillary geometry and estimates opacity UTC using the nearest same-sol UV timestamp and its spacecraft-clock offset.

To regenerate the archive, download [m20-ztau-merged-1717.txt](https://data.mendeley.com/public-files/datasets/gktjp9jptb/files/5eb62f12-eadf-4e87-b4bc-2966f7a5f00c/file_downloaded), then run `python data_processing/map/MEDA/export_weather.py <path-to-downloaded-table>`. The exporter checks the table's checksum, downloads the MEDA source products, and uses the existing `jezero-uv.json`.

## Solar UV radiation

In **Crater UV**, clicking a location also shows a simulated direct-beam UV Index. It uses a reference morning at about 08:15 local true solar time, corresponding to the solar geometry at 2022-11-11 10:51:16 UTC, with an assumed vertical UV optical depth of 0.5. The Sun angle and Mars–Sun distance come from Mars24. A ray through the 100 m CTX terrain checks whether a ridge blocks the direct beam. Where terrain coverage is insufficient, the panel shows a data gap.

The model integrates the [TSIS-1 hybrid solar reference spectrum](https://lasp.colorado.edu/lisird/data/tsis1_hsrs_p1nm/) against the [CIE erythema action spectrum](https://files.cie.co.at/cie209_2014.pdf) from 250 to 400 nm. It scales the result using the inverse square of the Mars–Sun distance, projects it onto a horizontal plane, and applies Beer–Lambert attenuation with a wavelength-independent optical depth. The UV Index is 40 times the resulting erythemally weighted irradiance in W/m². A zero index means the direct beam is blocked. The model omits diffuse UV and wavelength-dependent atmospheric effects. The MEDA rover readings are separate inputs to the nearby-stop estimate.

The spectrum product is LASP LISIRD `tsis1_hsrs_p1nm`, referenced to 1 AU. Its 0.1 nm spectral bandwidth is sampled every 0.025 nm in the integrated interval. Regenerate the spectral integral with `python data_processing/map/MEDA/export_uv_index_reference.py <tsis1_hsrs_p1nm.csv>`.

Open **Crater UV** and click the map. Blue dots show 14 Perseverance stops. Selecting a dot displays its measured average, and selecting a location near at least two stops displays an estimate. The estimate averages up to three stops within 5 km, weighting each site mean by the inverse of its great-circle distance. The map lists the sites, their distances, and reading counts. Stops follow the rover's western traverse, so much of the eastern crater has insufficient coverage. The estimate describes historical rover detector readings and atmospheric conditions can vary between stops.

The 14 site means use 55,933 calibrated MEDA TOP 3 readings (250–400 nm) from July 2021 through December 2024. Site means span 0.06275–0.11531 W/m². The exporter joins calibrated RDS readings to derived ANCILLARY geometry using spacecraft clock and local true solar time. It requires a stationary rover, a 35–45° solar zenith angle, a combined pitch and roll magnitude of at most 15°, and at least 60 valid readings within 100 m of each selected [NASA traverse waypoint](https://mars.nasa.gov/mmgis-maps/M20/Layers/json/M20_waypoints.json). It uses the mean position of the accepted samples for the site marker. The minimum distance between site markers is 729 m. All 14 sites fall on valid crater DEM cells and within the western CTX map. Eight also fall inside the HiRISE detail footprint.

The [NASA PDS MEDA archive](https://doi.org/10.17189/1522849) provides the source observations at roughly 1 Hz during instrument sessions. Detector orientation, dust and season remain sources of variation even for similar solar zenith angles. The site JSON records product identifiers, versions, source URLs, approximate UTC intervals and measurement counts. Rebuild it with `python data_processing/map/MEDA/export_uv_sites.py <product-directory>` after downloading the contributing CSVs listed in `frontend/public/data/map/MEDA/jezero-uv-sites.json` and their labels from the same URLs with the `.xml` extension.

In the separate western 2D map, **Show solar UV radiation** displays the previously sampled readings at one rover stop on a 0–0.2 W/m² scale. Observed values in that archive range from 0.0022 to 0.1971 W/m².

In that 2D map, **Show terrain UV simulation** displays relative direct-beam exposure for the selected archived time. The simulation uses the selected observation's approximate UTC to calculate the Sun's position using the [NASA GISS Mars24 ephemeris](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/tools/mars24/help/algorithm.html), adapted to the spherical Mars datum. Tests against four archived [MEDA ancillary geometry](https://atmos.nmsu.edu/PDS/data/PDS4/Mars2020/mars2020_meda/data_derived_env/sol_0540_0659/sol_0614/WE__0614___________DER_ANCILLARY___________P01.xml) readings on sol 614 found a maximum difference of 0.21° in solar zenith and 0.16° in azimuth at the rover.

Each model cell uses the cosine of the angle between the Sun and the surface normal. Terrain ray tracing checks whether a ridge or crater wall intercepts the direct beam. The model adjusts solar elevation across the crater, estimates curvature along each ray, and uses a shared solar bearing computed at the grid center. A surface facing the Sun with a clear direct beam scores 100%. The score is zero in a cast shadow or when the Sun is below the horizon. Uncolored terrain marks gaps or places where the model needs terrain outside the raster to evaluate a shadow. The raster extends from 77.00° to 78.58° E and 17.58° to 19.29° N. It samples the USGS 20 m [full-crater elevation mosaic](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_2020_science_investigation_ctx_dem_mosaic) at 100 m spacing. Regenerate the DEM and registered shaded relief with `python data_processing/map/CTX/export_crater_terrain.py`.

The 0–100% scale expresses geometric exposure to the direct beam. It omits dust attenuation, diffuse UV, fine terrain below the model grid scale, and ionizing radiation. The archived MEDA instrument readings have their own W/m² scale because the detector measures radiation in its field of view. The route planner uses terrain elevation for its calculations.

The compact dataset contains 142 daylight snapshots across Nov 11–18, 2022 UTC, all at one rover site near 18.453644° N, 77.399154° E. The site lies within both the CTX study area and the HiRISE detail footprint. Its time variation can reflect the solar angle, dust, and the sensor's orientation and limited viewing angle. The 2022 window supplies all eight requested dates from the published archive.
